Photo Recovery: File & Video – Privacy Policy (Amazon Appstore)

Last updated: October 24, 2026 · Developer: AlgoTechApps

Photo Recovery scans your Fire tablet or Fire TV for deleted or lost photos, videos, audio and documents (the system recycle bin, thumbnail caches, hidden folders, trash folders and app caches), lets you preview them and restores the ones you choose. All scanning and recovery happens on your device.

No ads, no internet, no purchases

The Amazon Appstore version of Photo Recovery shows no ads, contains no advertising or analytics SDK, has no in-app purchases and does not request internet access. It does not send any data anywhere.

Your files

The scan reads file names, sizes, dates and the first bytes of files (to recognise pictures and videos that lost their extension) only to show you what can be recovered. Your photos, videos, audio and documents are never uploaded or shared by the app. Restored items from the recycle bin go back to their original album; other recovered files are saved on your device in the "Photo Recovery" folder of Pictures, Movies, Music or Download, where you can open, share or delete them. The scan results are kept in memory only and are discarded when you close the app.

Data we collect

We (the developer) do not collect, store or sell any personal information. The app has no account system, no analytics and no location tracking. Your settings are stored only on your device.

Permissions

  • All files access (Fire tablets with Fire OS 8 and newer) – the core feature: the deep scan reads hidden folders, trash folders and caches that the gallery does not show, and copies the files you choose back into your gallery. Files never leave the device.
  • Storage (Fire TV, and Fire tablets with older Fire OS) – the same scan where the system offers no "All files access" setting, and saving recovered files on older Fire OS versions.

Restoring items from the system recycle bin and deleting recovered files always ask for your confirmation in a system dialog.

Children

Photo Recovery is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.
