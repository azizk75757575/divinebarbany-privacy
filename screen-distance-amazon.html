Screen Distance: Kids Eye Guard – Privacy Policy (Amazon Appstore)

Last updated: November 11, 2026 · Developer: AlgoTechApps · This policy covers the version of the app distributed on the Amazon Appstore for Fire tablets.

Screen Distance helps parents protect their child's eyes. It uses the tablet's front camera to notice when the screen is held too close to the eyes, shows a friendly "move back" screen over other apps and pauses videos until the child moves back. Parents can also turn on 20-20-20 eye breaks and see how often the tablet came too close.

Summary

The app has no ads, no analytics, no account and no sign-in. We (the developer) do not collect, store, share or sell any personal information, from parents or from children.

Camera

While eye protection is on, the app looks at a small, low-resolution picture from the front camera a few times per second. The tablet itself finds the eyes in that picture and measures the gap between them, which gives the distance to the screen. Each picture is checked in the tablet memory and thrown away at once. No photo or video is ever saved, uploaded or sent anywhere, and no face is recognised or identified: the app only knows how far the eyes are, not whose eyes they are. The camera is not used while the screen is off.

Showing over other apps

With your permission ("show over other apps"), the app shows the "move back" screen and the eye-break screen on top of the app your child is using. While this screen shows, the app briefly asks other apps to pause their sound, so videos pause. The app does not read what is on the screen or which app is open.

Background service and notifications

While eye protection is on, the app runs a foreground service with a visible notification, so the distance can be checked over every app. It starts again after the tablet restarts or the app is updated. You turn eye protection on and off yourself in the app.

Data stored on the tablet

The app saves only on the tablet: your settings, the calibration for your child (one number: the eye gap), the parent PIN as a one-way hash (never the PIN itself), and daily counts (number of warnings, minutes too close, minutes looking at the screen, eye breaks) for the last 30 days. None of this is ever uploaded. Uninstalling the app removes it.

In-app purchase

Premium can be unlocked with a one-time in-app purchase. The purchase is handled entirely by Amazon through the Amazon Appstore; we never see payment details. The app only receives from Amazon whether the purchase is owned and keeps that on the tablet. Amazon's handling of your data is described in the Amazon Privacy Notice.

Permissions

  • Camera – to measure the distance between the eyes and the screen, on the tablet only.
  • Show over other apps – to show the "move back" and eye-break screens over videos and games.
  • Foreground service, notifications and start at boot – to keep eye protection running and bring it back after a restart.
  • Internet – only used by the Amazon in-app purchasing service. The app itself sends nothing.

The app does not request access to the microphone, location, photos, contacts, messages or call log.

Children

Screen Distance is set up by a parent and is meant to protect children while they use the tablet. It does not collect, store or share any personal information from children or anyone else: no pictures, no names, no identifiers, no usage data leaves the tablet. There are no ads, no links to social media and no chat. Settings and the purchase screen can be protected with a parent PIN.

Not a medical device

Screen Distance helps children build a healthy habit. It is not a medical device and does not replace a visit to an eye doctor.
