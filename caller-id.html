Caller ID: Spam Block & Lookup – Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 25, 2026 · Developer: AlgoTechApps

Caller ID shows information about incoming calls from numbers that are not in your contacts, blocks the calls that match your rules, and lets you look up a phone number. Everything happens on your device: the app has no server and no shared database of phone numbers.

Incoming calls

When you make Caller ID your phone's "Caller ID & spam app", Android passes each incoming call's phone number to the app so it can be checked against your rules (block list, reported spam, allow list, and the switches you turned on). The check runs on your device in a fraction of a second. The app keeps its own list of the calls it screened (number, time, and whether it was allowed or blocked) in its private storage on your device, so you can see them under History. Calls from your contacts are not listed. You can delete any entry or clear the whole list at any time. This list is never uploaded and is not included in Android backups. Caller ID does not read your phone's call log.

Number information

The country, area, carrier and line type shown for a number come from public numbering-plan data stored inside the app (Google's open-source libphonenumber). Looking up a number does not send it anywhere. Only if you tap "Search web" is the number sent to your web browser to search for it, and only if you tap "WhatsApp", "Call" or "Message" is it handed to that app.

Contacts

If you allow access to your contacts, the app reads them on your device only, to show a contact's name and to let calls from your contacts through when you choose "Block numbers not in contacts". Your contacts are never copied, stored, uploaded or shared.

Your lists

Your block list, reported-spam list, allow list and recent lookups are stored only on your device. "Report spam" marks a number on your phone only; it is not shared with anyone.

Advertising

Caller ID is free and shows ads served by Google AdMob inside the app. Ads are never shown on the caller ID card or in notifications. To show and measure ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK may collect and process data such as the advertising ID, IP address (approximate location), device information, app interactions with ads and diagnostic data. This is governed by Google's privacy policy: policies.google.com/privacy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Where required (for example in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, and in some US states), the app asks for your consent with Google's consent message before personalised ads are shown. You can change your choice at any time in Settings > Privacy options. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings.

Caller ID has no in-app purchases. You can turn ads off for 24 hours by watching a short rewarded video; this setting is stored only on your device.

Data we collect

We (the developer) do not collect, store or sell any personal information. The app has no account system and no analytics of its own. Phone numbers, contacts, your lists and your call history never leave your device. The only data processed by third parties is the advertising data described above.

Permissions

  • Caller ID & spam app (Android role) – lets the app see incoming calls and block the ones that match your rules.
  • Contacts – optional: shows contact names and allows "Block numbers not in contacts".
  • Display over other apps – optional: shows the caller ID card on the incoming call screen.
  • Phone – optional: closes the caller ID card when you answer or the call ends. The app does not make or answer calls.
  • Notifications – optional: caller information when the card cannot be shown, and alerts for blocked calls.
  • Internet – to load ads and the consent message. Screening and lookups work offline.

Caller ID does not request access to your call log or your SMS messages.

Children

Caller ID is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.
